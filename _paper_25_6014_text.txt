UW-IDVO: An Underwater Information-Driven Visual Odometry
Front-End
Abstract—Underwater visual SLAM on resource-constrained
Unmanned Underwater Vehicles is challenged by degraded im-
agery and high computational cost. This paper presents an
Underwater Information-Driven Visual Odometry (UW-IDVO)
front-end that improves runtime and memory efficiency while
maintaining reliable pose accuracy. UW-IDVO consists of an
Information-Driven Point Selection (IDPS) strategy and an
Information-Driven Keyframe Decision (IDKD) strategy. IDPS
builds a pose Fisher information matrix from reprojection
Jacobians, assigns each map point match an information score
and a uniformity score, and fuses them into a selection score to
retain selected informative points for pose optimization. IDKD
makes keyframe decisions based on information variation using
an adaptive threshold and a cumulative degradation check, which
suppresses redundant keyframes and bounds local optimization
growth. We integrate UW-IDVO into the monocular ORB-
SLAM3 front-end and evaluate it on AQUALOC and EuRoC,
where experiments demonstrate its effectiveness and robustness.
We will release our open-source code to the community.
Index Terms —Underwater SLAM, Visual Odometry,
Information-Driven, Lightweight.
I. I NTRODUCTION
A
CCURATE self-localization and navigation are crucial
for Unmanned Underwater Vehicles (UUVs) to execute
complex tasks. Acoustic navigation sensors such as Doppler
Velocity Logs (DVLs) and Ultra-Short Baseline (USBL) sys-
tems provide reliable positioning, but their high cost, size,
and power consumption limit their use on small and low-cost
UUVs [1]. For resource-constrained UUVs, a compact low-
power camera paired with an inertial measurement unit offers
a more practical and scalable alternative. Consequently, visual
simultaneous localization and mapping (V-SLAM) has become
a promising solution for underwater autonomous navigation
[2].
Existing SLAM frameworks, such as VINS-Fusion [3] and
ORB-SLAM3 [4], perform well in ground and aerial appli-
cations. However, their performance drops significantly when
deployed on a resource-constrained UUV due to the combined
impact of environmental degradation and limited onboard
computation [5]. Underwater imaging quality is affected by
light absorption, scattering from suspended particles, color
distortion, low contrast, and motion blur. These factors make
it difficult to obtain stable visual observations, which often
lead to tracking failures. Meanwhile, underwater degradations
typically force the system to detect more observations to
maintain tracking. The front-end of a V-SLAM system is
already the most computationally demanding component [6].
On a resource-constrained UUV , handling a large number of
low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and redundant observations
wastes valuable computational resources. It also reduces the
resources available for back-end optimization and loop closure
detection, which may eventually cause the system to fail.

Recent research has made significant efforts to improve
the visibility of underwater images [7]. These approaches
aim to mitigate the degradation introduced by underwater
imaging conditions. They can enhance visual inputs to some
extent, but their computational cost remains non-negligible
on a resource-constrained UUV [8]. In addition, existing
lightweight solutions focus mainly on accelerating image
enhancement algorithms or compressing neural networks [9].
They lack optimizations that arise from the structure of V-
SLAM itself, such as map point selection strategies and
keyframe management. As a result, a key gap remains in
current research: how can we reduce the computational scale
of the visual odometry front-end so that V-SLAM remains
accurate and efficient on resource-constrained UUVs?
In this paper, we present an Underwater Information-Driven
Visual Odometry front-end (UW-IDVO), which is designed
to significantly reduce the size and computational load of
the front-end optimization while preserving pose accuracy.
A representative example is shown in Fig. 1, where UW-
IDVO matches the trajectory accuracy of the baseline while
substantially reducing the per-frame computational time and
lowering the overall memory usage. The main contributions

II. R ELATED WORK
A. Underwater Visual SLAM
Underwater SLAM research increasingly focuses on multi-
sensor fusion to mitigate the instability of visual perception
in harsh underwater environments. These approaches typi-
cally tightly fuse visual observations with inertial, pressure,
acoustic, magnetic, imaging sonar, or DVL data, enabling
more robust localization under low-visibility or highly turbid
conditions [12]–[16]. However, visual sensors provide rich
environmental information and therefore remain the primary
sensing modality in most underwater missions [17]. Most
existing work on underwater vision focuses on improving input
image quality. However, image enhancement still leaves many
noisy observations. The V-SLAM front-end must process a
large number of low-information map points, which leads to
unstable tracking and increases the burden of local bundle ad-
justment [18]. Existing methods lack mechanisms that evaluate
the quality of underwater visual observations from the front-
end perspective and proactively constrain the computational
scale.
B. Underwater Lightweight SLAM
Lightweight underwater SLAM methods can be broadly
categorized into front-end and back-end strategies [19]. Front-
end approaches typically reduce the volume of image data
to be processed by applying fast de-scattering, salient region
filtering, or lightweight enhancement models [20]. However,
these methods mainly improve the quality of the observa-
tions themselves and cannot eliminate the large number of
unstable measurements that the front-end must still handle
in degraded underwater environments. Back-end lightweight
strategies commonly rely on keyframe pruning, window size

information-driven mechanisms to the SLAM front-end in
underwater settings, aiming to control the computational scale
while improving overall system efficiency.
III. S YSTEM OVERVIEW
The ORB-SLAM family is widely used as a baseline
framework for underwater SLAM due to its robust and mature
architecture [26]. In this work, the proposed UW-IDVO is
integrated into the front-end of ORB-SLAM3. The overall
system structure is shown in Fig. 2.
Following the Track Local Map procedure in ORB-SLAM,
once an initial camera pose estimate and an initial set of feature
matches are available, the system projects a local map into
the frame and searches more map point matches. In the native
pipeline, the camera pose is refined by performing motion-
only bundle adjustment (motion-only BA) using all map point
matches found in the current frame. In our system, we insert
an IDPS module between local-map reprojection matching and
motion-only BA.
IDPS starts from the candidate set of points and computes
the corresponding reprojection Jacobians. These Jacobians are
used to form a Fisher information matrix that characterizes
how strongly the available measurements constrain the camera
pose. Based on this matrix, each candidate point is assigned an
information score that reflects its contribution to reducing pose
uncertainty. To avoid selecting points that cluster in highly
textured regions, we additionally compute a uniformity score

to rank all candidates and retain only a compact subset of
selected informative points, while the remaining points are
pruned so that they do not contribute residual terms in the

In addition, we introduce an IDKD strategy to regulate
keyframe creation and further reduce the back-end workload.
IDKD first evaluates the information variation between the
current frame and the last keyframe, then applies an adaptive
threshold to judge whether the change is significant under
the current tracking condition, and finally checks cumulative
degradation to capture persistent information loss over time.
These cues are jointly used to produce the final keyframe
decision, which avoids redundant keyframes and lowers the
computational cost of subsequent local mapping operations.
The proposed framework preserves the original data struc-
tures and module organization of ORB-SLAM3 and can be
implemented on top of the existing codebase with minimal
changes. By controlling the computational scale of the front-
end, the burden on the back-end optimization is directly
reduced and the overall system efficiency is improved.

C. Information-Driven Keyframe Decision
After obtaining the information matrix for each frame, we
apply an information-driven keyframe decision to determine
whether the current frame should be promoted to a keyframe.

ric constraints for subsequent pose estimation. IDKD triggers
keyframe insertion when the current frame yields a sufficiently
large information change relative to the last keyframe, or when
the information shows persistent degradation over time.

V. E XPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Setup
Baseline: UW-IDVO is integrated into the front-end of
ORB-SLAM3. To ensure a fair comparison, the original
monocular configuration of ORB-SLAM3 is used as the base-
line in all experiments.
Datasets: The Harbor sequences from the AQUALOC
dataset are adopted as the primary benchmark. Following
established practice, sequences 4 and 7 are excluded because
no quantitative evaluation is possible [10], [12]. Sequences
1 and 3 are used for parameter tuning, and independent
validation is carried out on sequences 1, 2, 3, 5, and 6 under
the same parameter settings. To further assess the generality of
the proposed approach beyond underwater environments, UW-
IDVO is also evaluated on the EuRoC MA V dataset, which
represents a well-calibrated scenario with stable illumination.
Metrics: Both trajectory accuracy and computational effi-
ciency are evaluated. Trajectory accuracy is evaluated using
Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error
(RPE), both reported as root-mean-square (RMSE) values.
Efficiency is assessed by the average tracking time per frame
(Tracking time), the average local bundle adjustment time
(Local BA time), the mean number of selected informative
points (#Points), the total number of generated keyframes
(#Keyframes), and the system memory usage (Memory).
Platform: All experiments were conducted on Ubuntu
20.04.6 LTS, and the proposed method was implemented in
C++. The hardware platform was a laptop equipped with an
Intel Core i5-13600KF CPU. To emulate resource-constrained
UUV platforms, the SLAM system was restricted to 4 CPU
cores and 8 GB of available memory during all experiments.
Execution protocol: All experiments are executed using
ROS bag online playback at the original recording rate, rather
than offline frame-by-frame processing, in order to better
replicate real UUV deployment conditions. This setup natu-
rally introduces non-deterministic timing and occasional frame
drops, making the evaluation more representative of practical
usage scenarios. Each experiment is repeated 10 times, and
the median of the results is reported to mitigate the effects of
runtime variability.
UW-IDVO is evaluated under four front-end configurations.
BASE denotes the original ORB-SLAM3 monocular front-end.
IDPS augments ORB-SLAM3 with the proposed IDPS module
only. IDKD augments ORB-SLAM3 with the proposed IDKD
module only. IDVO corresponds to the full UW-IDVO front-
end, where both modules are enabled.
B. Parameter Tuning
To systematically evaluate the behavior of the UW-IDVO
front-end under different parameter settings, we tune the
hyperparameters using sequences 1 and 3 of the AQUALOC
Harbor dataset. We first tune the hyperparameters under the
IDPS configuration, and then refine them under the IDVO
configuration. In what follows, we describe the selection
process for two key hyperparameters: the point budget ( κtop)
and the base threshold for information variation ( τ0).

local optimization, but does not significantly further improve
pose accuracy. Balancing accuracy, stability, and efficiency,
we choose κtop = 95 as a good trade-off: it lies on the
accuracy plateau while incurring clearly lower runtime than
larger settings. The higher memory usage in the low-budget
regime is further alleviated in the next step by the information-
driven keyframe decision strategy.
Fig. 4 shows the effect of the base threshold for information
variation τ0 on system performance. In practice, setting τ0
no larger than 2.4 ensures stable operation. As shown in
Fig. 4(a), both ATE and RPE remain low and stable within
the tested range, indicating that the system maintains sufficient
geometric constraints. Fig. 4(b) shows that the number of
keyframes drops sharply as τ0 increases, while the number
of selected points exhibits a mild downward trend. Fig. 4(c)
indicates that the runtimes of the tracking and local BA threads
decrease significantly as τ0 grows and become largely stable
after τ0 = 1.8, with only a slow further reduction. Fig. 4(d)
presents a similar trend for memory usage, where the benefit
of increasing τ0 saturates around τ0 = 1 .8. Overall, Fig. 4
suggests that both τ0 = 1.8 and τ0 = 2.2 provide a good trade-
off between accuracy and efficiency. While τ0 = 1 .8 yields
slightly better accuracy, τ0 = 2.2 achieves lower runtime and
memory usage. Since this work targets a lightweight SLAM
front-end for resource-constrained UUVs, we choose τ0 = 2.2
as the default setting in the following experiments.

C. Evaluation on AQUALOC Harbor
We evaluate UW-IDVO on the AQUALOC Harbor dataset,
which exhibits typical underwater imaging degradation and is
well suited for assessing both lightweight design and system
stability. Results are reported under four front-end configura-
tions: BASE, IDPS, IDKD, and IDVO.
Fig. 5 provides an intuitive view of how IDPS controls
the amount and the spatial distribution of constraints used for
pose estimation. Instead of retaining all candidate points, IDPS
selects a compact subset by ranking candidates with a selection
score that combines an information score to favor strong
geometric constraints and a uniformity score to discourage
locally redundant constraints from the same textured region.
This effect is most evident when many candidate points are
available (Fig. 5(a)–(b)), where the selected points remain
sparse and well distributed. The boxed regions in Fig. 5(c)–(d)
further illustrate that dense candidate point clusters are thinned
after selection, indicating that the uniformity score effectively
regularizes the selection. In sparse underwater scenes, candi-
date points can be unevenly distributed (Fig. 5(e)–(f)); in such
cases, IDPS becomes conservative and retains most (or all)
candidate points to avoid under-constrained pose estimation.
Fig. 6 complements these observations at the sequence level:
compared with the baseline, UW-IDVO produces trajecto-

configurations on five Harbor sequences. Overall, IDPS tends

the tracking time by 20.6%. Consequently, IDVO provides

D. Evaluation on EuRoC
We further evaluate UW-IDVO on the EuRoC MH se-
quences, which provide high-quality imagery with aggressive
motion and rich texture, to assess generalization beyond un-
derwater environments.
Table III reports the quantitative results under BASE and
IDVO. From the efficiency perspective, IDVO reduces the
average local BA time by 83.4% and decreases memory usage
by 26.7%, indicating a substantially smaller local optimization
workload and a more compact map. In contrast, the per-
frame tracking time changes only slightly ( +6.1%), because
tracking on EuRoC is mainly dominated by feature extraction,
matching, and local-map projection, while the proposed mech-
anisms primarily reduce the constraints entering optimization
and limit the growth of the local mapping window. Comput-
ing information-based scores also introduces extra overhead,
which can offset part of the tracking-side savings when the
baseline tracking is already efficient.
From the accuracy perspective, IDVO remains comparable
to BASE, with only minor changes in the average ATE
(+8.5%) and RPE ( +5.9%). This mild increase is expected
in well-conditioned EuRoC sequences, because the images
already contain abundant high-quality features and the base-
line front-end is sufficiently constrained. In this case, further
pruning and a more compact local optimization window may
slightly reduce constraint redundancy and lead to a small loss
in accuracy. By contrast, on AQUALOC Harbor, underwater
degradation introduces many low SNR and unstable observa-
tions, and IDVO is more beneficial because it suppresses noisy
constraints and improves the effective quality of measure-
ments. Overall, these results show that the proposed front-end
improves efficiency while preserving near-baseline accuracy
across both underwater and non-underwater datasets.

VI. C ONCLUSION
In this paper, we presented UW-IDVO, an underwater
information-driven visual odometry front-end tailored for
resource-constrained UUVs. By integrating IDPS and IDKD to
select informative map point constraints and regulate keyframe
growth, UW-IDVO leads to a smaller and bounded local opti-
mization problem, improving runtime and memory efficiency
while maintaining reliable pose accuracy.
UW-IDVO is integrated into the monocular ORB-SLAM3
front-end and evaluated on the AQUALOC Harbor and Eu-
RoC MH datasets. On Harbor, UW-IDVO preserves trajectory
accuracy while reducing the average per-frame tracking and
local BA time by about 83% and lowering map memory usage
by about 36% compared with the baseline. On EuRoC MH,
UW-IDVO continues to substantially reduce the local BA time
by about 83% and decreases memory usage by about 27%,
while maintaining stable trajectories with only mild changes
in absolute accuracy.
UW-IDVO offers an approach to a lightweight visual odom-
etry front-end. Future work will extend this information-driven
design toward multi-sensor SLAM and adaptive computation
allocation.
